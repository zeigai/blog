Trading more often can feel like progress. Every new position looks like another opportunity, and sitting on the sidelines can feel like missing out. 

The evidence suggests otherwise. Frequent trading can increase costs, expose traders to more decisions under pressure and, in some cases, reflect overconfidence rather than a clear trading edge. More traders can mean more activity without better results. 

Overtrading occurs when traders place more trades than their strategy, risk limits or available market opportunities justify. It is not necessarily about the number of trades, but whether each trade has a valid reason behind it. 

## **The costs that quietly add up** 

Every trade carries a cost, even when it is not obvious. Depending on the market and account type, that can include the spread, commissions, overnight financing charges and slippage when orders are filled at a worse price that expected. 

On a single trade, these costs can look small. Across many traders, however, they accumulate and can significantly reduce net returns. Consider a hypothetical trader who pays a total of $50 daily, or around $1,000 over twenty trading days, before a single price movement is taken into account. The strategy must earn back those costs before it makes anything at all.
